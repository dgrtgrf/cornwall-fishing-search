## Method and honest limits
- WebSearch budget for the whole run was EXHAUSTED after 2 of ~6 planned queries (tool refused further searches); I did not work around it. seaangler.co.uk (Cloudflare 403 to curl and WebFetch), pressreader and everand (403 / JS-wall) could not be read; their content appears here only as search-engine snippets, marked.
- Backbone of this file: the Where's The Fish (wtf) Cornwall mark database (grade C: app with templated, apparently model-written summaries; no named authors, no dates; per-mark text is formulaic - the SAME phrases recur - so treat species/tide/light fields as low-evidence description, not catch data). Its coordinates and 'shoreline aspect' fields are structured data in the page (coord_confidence=medium: mark pin, not verified against survey).
- Corroboration (grade B): British Sea Fishing SW guide (raw/N1), UKBASS 'Surf angling for bass in Cornwall' (BASS magazine 1984, repub 2005), and a Sea Angler 'Down Your Way' 9 Feb 2017 snippet (Tregantle/Freathy/Portwrinkle).
- Grade A: BRFC June 2026 record list (raw/T1/brfc_june2026.txt).
- wtf 'in season' lists on 21 Nov 2026 are an app model output (C) - NOT observed catches. All mark-by-mark 'late_nov_species' use that list; inference (D) noted where I add to it.

## Cross-cutting evidence
- UKBASS (B, 1984/2005): best Cornish surf bass conditions come later in the year (autumn depressions); Atlantic swell giving 'an even surf with deep water tables' is the most important factor; poor in calm clear water; avoid gales / more than ~Force 6 with a big tide; best 2h either side of LOW water, fish up to HW; springs > neaps; gullies near low spring tide mark are 'natural food traps', streams running into the surf too; sand ground, weed can collect in gullies at low water; sandeel 'undoubtedly the best bait', also blast-frozen sandeel, lug, razor, mackerel strip, soft crab. Named bass beaches: Widemouth, Constantine, Watergate, Perran Sands, Hayle Sands (up to 3 miles - choose position), Sennen Cove. Looe estuary lugworm digging.
  DISAGREEMENT: UKBASS says best around LOW water; wtf says nearly every beach is best on the FLOOD into dusk/HW. Not averaged. (Likely method difference: low-water fishing the gutters vs high-water fishing the troughs; D.)
- BSF (B): Whitsand Bay great for bass 'especially when some sea is running'; in front of Tregantle Fort 'best for large bass'; Widemouth small-eyed + thornback ray to sandeel/mackerel at range, bass just behind breakers, plaice, sole, turbot possible; Watergate bass when sea running, rays to long casts; Porthmeor (St Ives) large bass when sea running, ray chance, some winter cod; St Austell Bay/Pentewan good flounder and bass; Carne cleaner ground for bass and flatfish; Looe river winter whiting/flounder/cod.
- Sea Angler 2017 (B, snippet only): Tregantle bass and rays, 'very little during daylight' = night mark; casting beyond the breakers reaches big bass when a big sea is running.
- BRFC (A): NO Cornish surf-beach entry in the shore record list for bass, small-eyed/blonde/thornback/spotted rays, turbot, brill, plaice, cod, golden-grey mullet, sole. 'Whitsands Bay' 8lb10oz is the BOAT record for SPOTTED ray (1998, A Hodge) - a search-engine summary mis-called it a shore record (contradiction noted). Cornish shore entries in the list are rock/harbour/estuary fish (e.g. Pemberth Cove 21lb13oz 2022, Porthallow 52lb11oz 1980 ray, Helford River, Porthcurno, Newlyn Harbour) - none from a surf beach.
- wtf catch logs (C, Oct-Nov 2026): only 13 beach/rock marks have any catch; beach ones: Crinnis (bass 52 cm released 4 Oct 00:31 paddle-tail, bass 38 cm 27 Sep ragworm, bull huss 12 Oct), Par (bass 34 cm 14 Oct and one on 16 Oct on sandeel soft plastic), Porthallow (ballan wrasse). All lure/LRF dated Sep-Oct; NONE in November on a beach. THIN DATA.

## Late-November realism (inference D from the above)
Realistic species on open Cornish beaches late Nov: whiting (every source), dogfish/huss, flounder (esp. river-mouth beaches: Par, Pentewan, Seaton, Crinnis, Perranporth, Crantock/Gannel, Hayle, Rock/Daymer), dabs, pouting, occasional codling on storm shingle (Loe Bar - the only cod-named beach), bass only if sea is running and water temperature allows (wtf lists bass in-season but gives no dated Nov catch); small-eyed rays, turbot, brill, mullet, sole and gilthead are summer-autumn species - no documentation of any of them on a Cornish beach in late November. Steep-shingle marks with deep water close in (Loe Bar, Porthleven, Gunwalloe/Church Cove, Seaton-Downderry, Vault/Porthoustock/Godrevy-Lizard) are described as storm/onshore marks; the 22 Nov sea state is for other streams to say.
